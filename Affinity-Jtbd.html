Affinity Map + JTBD  ·  CURATE

From evidence to needs

How 21 survey responses were sorted into recurring themes, then translated into evidence-backed needs — without skipping straight to a feature.

User needs synthesis · August 2026 · n = 21

The affinity map

Every structured and open-ended response was sorted into seven recurring themes. Each theme card kept participant wording visually distinct from my own synthesis, so a theme summary never got mistaken for a direct quote or a product requirement.

Theme 01 Discovery is social and often passive

Most people encounter fashion inspiration while scrolling, not while actively searching — and social platforms, especially TikTok, dominate.

Theme 02 Personal relevance matters

Visual appeal gets first attention, but whether something fits their actual style is a close second reason to stop.

Theme 03 Price, fit & representation shape relevance

Affordability and body/size representation come up as often as style fit when people judge whether inspiration is "for them."

Theme 04 Saving is a recurring part of the journey

Bookmarking is the single most common action in the entire survey — the pivot between discovery and later intent.

Theme 05 Discovery often leads to more research

People want styling context and examples after finding a look, before they're ready to act on it.

Theme 06 Trust & trend recognition are socially reinforced

Repeated exposure and friends outweigh institutional "trending" labels as credibility signals.

Theme 07 Repetition, ads & traceability create friction

Homogenous algorithmic content, ad clutter, and untraceable items are the top complaints, in that order.

Evidence-backed user needs

Directional needs derived from survey evidence. Needs are problem-level — proposed features remain open. Use these to guide MVP prioritization and information architecture, not as proof of a specific UI solution.

01Discover relevant inspiration without knowing exactly what to search for
Evidence from n = 21
  • 10/21 named TikTok as their single main discovery source
  • 9/21 said trends are usually discovered while scrolling or browsing
  • 7/21 reported a mix of passive discovery and active searching
User need

People need to encounter useful fashion inspiration even when they do not begin with a specific trend name or search query.

JTBD statement

When I want fashion inspiration but do not know exactly what I am looking for, I want to browse relevant looks so I can discover ideas that interest me.

Do not assume yet

This does not yet prove the product needs a TikTok-style feed or recommendation algorithm.

02Judge whether inspiration is personally relevant
Evidence from n = 21
  • 14/21 stop because the outfit or style catches their attention
  • 12/21 stop when it fits their personal style
  • 10/21 consider body type/size; 10/21 consider affordability
User need

People need to quickly assess whether fashion inspiration is relevant to their style, body, and budget.

JTBD statement

When I see a look that interests me, I want to understand whether it fits my style, body, and budget so I can decide whether it is useful to me.

Do not assume yet

This does not yet prove which filters, profile fields, or personalization controls should exist.

03Save inspiration and return to it later
Evidence from n = 21
  • 15/21 selected save/bookmark after finding a style they like
  • 14/21 save inspiration regularly or sometimes
  • Pinterest boards (12) and TikTok favorites (9) are common saving locations
User need

People need a way to preserve useful fashion inspiration so they can revisit it later.

JTBD statement

When I find a look I may want later, I want to save it in a way I can find again so I can return when I am ready to style, research, or shop.

Do not assume yet

This does not yet prove the product needs Pinterest-style boards or a specific collection system.

04Understand how a trend or look can actually be worn
Evidence from n = 21
  • 12/21 want information on how to style a trend
  • 11/21 want photos or examples of the trend
  • 7/21 want to know what the trend is called
User need

People need practical context that helps them understand and apply fashion inspiration.

JTBD statement

When I discover a trend or look I like, I want to see clear examples and styling context so I can understand how I might wear it myself.

Do not assume yet

This does not yet prove how much editorial content a trend page should contain.

05Move from inspiration to useful item information
Evidence from n = 21
  • 7/21 search for the clothing/items after finding a style they like
  • 7/21 want to know where to buy similar items
  • 5 respondents reported difficulty finding the actual clothing/items
User need

People need a clear path from inspiration to information about the items or similar options when they choose to investigate further.

JTBD statement

When I want to act on a look I found, I want to identify the item or find similar options so I do not have to restart the search elsewhere.

Do not assume yet

This does not establish shopping as the product's primary purpose or prove that checkout should happen on-platform.

06Understand why something is being presented as a trend
Evidence from n = 21
  • 15/21 view repeated social-media exposure as a signal of a trend
  • 11/21 use seeing people around them wearing it as a trend signal
  • 15/21 selected friends/people they know among trusted style sources
User need

People need enough context to understand why something is being presented as current or trending.

JTBD statement

When something is labeled as a trend, I want enough context to judge whether that label feels credible and relevant before I pay attention to it.

Do not assume yet

This does not yet prove which trend score, social proof, or source indicators should be shown.

Research boundary: exploratory survey only, n = 21. Short answers are not equivalent to interviews — every need above is directional, not validated.
